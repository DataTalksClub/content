We checked the rules and pages on 24 November 2026. For questions about your own case, ask the Agentur für Arbeit.

## Week 1: The Two Registrations

You need to register twice, in two separate steps. The Agentur für Arbeit is clear about this: registering as a jobseeker doesn't count as registering as unemployed.

First, register as a jobseeker (arbeitsuchend melden). You have to do this no later than three months before your job ends. If you find out about the end date less than three months in advance, you have three days from the day you find out. This rule is in § 38 Abs. 1 SGB III, and it applies even if you're fighting the dismissal in court or your employer hints that you might be able to stay ([§ 38 SGB III](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/sgb_3/__38.html)).

You can register online, by phone at the free service number 0800 4 555500, or in person. The Agentur says online is the easiest way, and asks you to use only one of these options ([Wie Sie sich arbeitsuchend melden](https://www.arbeitsagentur.de/arbeitslos-arbeit-finden/arbeitslosengeld/ihre-schritte-wenn-sie-arbeitslos-werden/wie-sie-sich-arbeitsuchend-melden)).

Second, register as unemployed (arbeitslos melden). You can do this up to three months before you become unemployed, and no later than your first day without work. Arbeitslosengeld is paid from the day you register at the earliest, so if you register late, you lose the benefit for those days ([Wie Sie sich arbeitslos melden](https://www.arbeitsagentur.de/arbeitslos-arbeit-finden/arbeitslosengeld/ihre-schritte-wenn-sie-arbeitslos-werden/wie-sie-sich-arbeitslos-melden)).

You can register as unemployed online or in person ([§ 141 SGB III](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/sgb_3/__141.html)). To register online, you identify yourself with the online function of your ID card, your electronic residence permit or an eID card, and you need the PIN for it. In person, bring your ID card with your current address, or a passport with a current Meldebescheinigung.

If you register as a jobseeker too late, you get a one-week Sperrzeit: a week in which you get no Arbeitslosengeld ([§ 159 Abs. 6 SGB III](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/sgb_3/__159.html), and the Bundesagentur's [press release of 3 September 2026](https://www.arbeitsagentur.de/en/press/2026-33-register-as-a-jobseeker-in-good-time-make-the-most-of-opportunities-and-avoid-a-waiting-period)).

## Week 1: Before You Sign Anything

In many layoffs, the employer offers you an agreement to leave, often with a severance payment. In German the agreement is an Aufhebungsvertrag, and the payment is an Abfindung.

Be careful here. The Bundesagentur lists an Aufhebungsvertrag (for example one with a severance payment) and your own resignation among the reasons for a Sperrzeit. If you give up a job, the Sperrzeit can last up to 12 weeks without Arbeitslosengeld. You can avoid it if you have an important reason and can prove it ([Arbeitslosengeld: Anspruch, Höhe, Dauer](https://www.arbeitsagentur.de/arbeitslos-arbeit-finden/arbeitslosengeld/finanzielle-hilfen/arbeitslosengeld-anspruch-hoehe-dauer), [§ 159 SGB III](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/sgb_3/__159.html)).

Before you sign, ask the Agentur für Arbeit or an employment lawyer what the agreement means for your Arbeitslosengeld. Waiting a few days costs you much less than three months without Arbeitslosengeld.

## Week 2: Arbeitslosengeld in Short

You can get Arbeitslosengeld if all of these are true ([Arbeitslosengeld: Anspruch, Höhe, Dauer](https://www.arbeitsagentur.de/arbeitslos-arbeit-finden/arbeitslosengeld/finanzielle-hilfen/arbeitslosengeld-anspruch-hoehe-dauer)):

- you have no job, but you can work at least 15 hours a week in a job with social insurance
- you registered as unemployed
- you're looking for a job and working with the Agentur für Arbeit on it
- you were insured for at least 12 months in the last 30 months (the Anwartschaftszeit in [§ 142 SGB III](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/sgb_3/__142.html), within the Rahmenfrist of 30 months in [§ 143 SGB III](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/sgb_3/__143.html))

You get 60 percent of your net daily wage as the Agentur calculates it, or 67 percent if you or your spouse or partner have at least one child. The Agentur calculates it from your gross salary over the last 12 months. If you're under 50, you get it for up to 12 months, as long as you were insured for at least 24 months (same [Bundesagentur page](https://www.arbeitsagentur.de/arbeitslos-arbeit-finden/arbeitslosengeld/finanzielle-hilfen/arbeitslosengeld-anspruch-hoehe-dauer)).

Registering as unemployed isn't the same as applying for the money. You apply for Arbeitslosengeld separately, and you can do that online.

## Week 2: If You Are on a Residence Permit Tied to Your Job

If your residence permit depends on your job, for example an EU Blue Card, losing the job affects your permit too. The rules depend on the type of permit and how long you've had it, so don't guess. Contact your Ausländerbehörde early, and read the official [questions and answers about the EU Blue Card](https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/pdf-questions-answers-eu-blue-card) on Make it in Germany, the federal government's portal for skilled workers.

## Week 3: Your Documents and Your Story

Once the paperwork is done, get together what you'll need for every application:

- Your CV: update it while you still remember the details of your last projects.
- Your reference letter (Arbeitszeugnis): in Germany you have the right to a written reference when you leave ([§ 109 GewO](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/gewo/__109.html)). Ask for it early, and read it before you accept it.
- What you shipped: write down the systems you built, the problems you solved and the numbers you can share. You'll need this in interviews, and it's easiest to write it down now.

Then prepare a short story about yourself: why you're looking, what you do best, and what you want to do next. You'll tell it in every first call.

## Week 4: How to Use the Gap

A gap on a CV isn't a problem if you can explain what you did with the time.

You can use it in several ways, and combine them:

- Job search: networking and referrals work better than mass applications. I wrote about this in the [guide on getting hired as an experienced developer](/academy/guides/getting-hired-with-ai/).
- Free courses in your own time, while you keep looking for work.
- Short workshops for one specific skill.
- A full-time program of about three months, if you want to change how you work.

Many developer job ads now ask for AI skills. We counted developer jobs in Germany on arbeitnow in the week of 7 to 14 November 2026: 86 of 588 ads named Claude Code, Cursor, Copilot, Codex or AI-assisted coding. It's an approximate count, since we just searched the ad texts for these terms.

## Training While You Are Unemployed: What Exists

The Agentur für Arbeit can fund further training with a Bildungsgutschein (education voucher). You have no general right to it. The Agentur decides case by case whether you need the training to find work again ([§ 81 SGB III](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/sgb_3/__81.html), [Förderung der beruflichen Weiterbildung](https://www.arbeitsagentur.de/karriere-und-weiterbildung/foerderung-berufliche-weiterbildung)). ## German Terms You Will See

These words show up on forms and in letters from the Agentur:

- arbeitsuchend: looking for work. You register as arbeitsuchend while you still have your job but know it's ending.
- arbeitslos: unemployed. You register as arbeitslos at the latest on your first day without work.
- Arbeitslosengeld (ALG I): unemployment benefit, paid from unemployment insurance.
- Sperrzeit: a period without Arbeitslosengeld, for example after a late jobseeker registration or after giving up a job.
- Aufhebungsvertrag: an agreement with your employer to end the job, often with a severance payment (Abfindung).
- Agentur für Arbeit: the local employment agency of the Bundesagentur für Arbeit. It handles registration and Arbeitslosengeld.
- Jobcenter: the office for basic income support, for people who don't get Arbeitslosengeld or whose Arbeitslosengeld isn't enough to live on.

## FAQ

### When Do I Have to Register as a Jobseeker After a Layoff in Germany?

No later than three months before your job ends. If you find out about the end date less than three months in advance, you have three days from the day you find out.

### Is Registering as a Jobseeker the Same as Registering as Unemployed?

No, they're two separate steps. You register as unemployed no later than your first day without a job, and Arbeitslosengeld is paid from the day you register at the earliest.

### Can I Study While I Am Unemployed in Germany?

You can always take free courses in your own time. For full-time training, talk to the Agentur für Arbeit first: to get Arbeitslosengeld you have to stay available for work, and if you arrange training yourself, you only count as available if the Agentur agrees to it ([§ 139 Abs. 3 SGB III](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/sgb_3/__139.html)).
