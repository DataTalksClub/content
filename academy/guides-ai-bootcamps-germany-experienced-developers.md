## What "AI Bootcamp" Means in Germany

In Germany, people use "AI bootcamp" for three quite different things:

1. Long programs for career starters: several months full-time, often starting from programming basics, for people who want to get into tech.
2. Short courses without code: a few days on what AI can do for a business. Some of the top search results for "AI bootcamp" in Germany are this kind of course, for example a 5-day course with no coding, or live workshops for people in finance.
3. Programs for working developers: you need to be able to program already, and you spend the time building with LLMs and coding agents. There are only a few of these.

If you already work as a developer, look at the entry level first. Length, language and format matter less.

## AI Bootcamps for Career Starters

Ironhack says you don't need any technical experience for its AI Engineering bootcamp. You do 40+ hours of prework, then 9 weeks full-time or 24 weeks part-time, live online in English.

Le Wagon says you don't need technical experience or programming knowledge for its AI Software bootcamp in Berlin, as long as you do a 40-hour prep course first. It takes 2 months full-time or 6 months part-time, in person in Berlin or online.

WBS Coding School says most of its courses are designed for beginners and career changers. Its AI Software Development course takes 17 weeks full-time, Monday to Friday, online, in English or German. It also lists programs of 10 or 12 months.

These programs are a good fit if you're new to tech. If you have years of experience, you'd spend much of the time on things you already do at work.

## AI Bootcamps for Developers Who Already Code

For a few programs, you need some programming experience before you start:

- For Spiced Academy's AI Engineering program, you need programming skills, ideally in Python, and you should be comfortable with numbers, algebra and statistics. It takes 32 weeks full-time, in Berlin or online. neue fische offers the same 32-week AI Engineering program. Its page names basic programming as a requirement, but also says you don't need an IT background.
- For Le Wagon's Data Science & AI bootcamp, you need basic programming and maths. It takes 2 months full-time.
- Digitale Leute School says that ideally you already have some practical experience in software development, but you don't need to be a senior engineer. The full-time format is meant for newcomers and career changers, and the part-time format for people who study next to their job.
- Turing College wants at least a year of Python or JavaScript for its AI Engineering program, and says it's for developers who want to use their coding skills to build LLM applications and agents. It's self-paced, with no live lectures, and takes 3 to 4 months.

So "for people who already code" usually means a year of experience or basic programming, not several years of professional work.

## AI Courses for Experienced Developers in Germany

If you have several years of experience and want to learn AI skills, you can sort the options into three groups by length:

- Short workshops of 2 to 5 days are good for an overview or one tool, but too short to build and evaluate a real project.
- Programs of about 3 months: Digitale Leute School, Turing College and DataTalks.Club Academy are in this group. Check whether the teaching is live or self-paced, and what experience the other people in the group have.
- Long programs of 8 months or more: Spiced Academy and neue fische take 32 weeks for AI Engineering, and WBS Coding School lists programs of 10 or 12 months. You cover a lot of topics, but it takes up a large part of a year.

## Short Courses and Workshops

artificialintelligencebootcamp.de runs a 5-day AI bootcamp in Frankfurt or as a live webinar, and says there's no coding and anyone can join. zeb.business school runs AI bootcamp workshops for staff of financial institutions.

These courses help managers and business teams understand AI. They don't teach a developer how to build, test and ship an LLM application.

## How to Choose by Experience Level

It mostly depends on how long you've worked as a developer:

- Under 2 years as a developer: a career-starter program or a free course is the right fit. The free [DataTalks.Club Zoomcamps](/courses) are a good place to start, for example [LLM Zoomcamp](/courses/llm-zoomcamp).
- 2+ years as a developer: look for a program that skips the basics and focuses on building with LLMs or coding agents. Ask who else is in the group.
- Team leads: you may not need a full program. A short workshop for the overview, plus time to build one project yourself, can be enough. If you want to get hands-on again, a 3-month program makes sense.

## Questions to Ask Any Bootcamp Before You Sign

- What level are the other participants at? Ask what most of them did before the program.
- Who teaches? One instructor every day, or rotating teachers and mentors?
- How big is the group?
- What do you build? Ask to see projects from earlier participants.
- Is it live or self-paced? Recorded videos with office hours aren't the same as live teaching every day.
